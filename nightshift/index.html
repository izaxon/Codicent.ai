night shift

They come and go. The memory stays.

One person, and a company that keeps working when he goes home. At ten at night Johan Isaksson switches off the lamp. Through the night small blue squares build, test, merge and draft the invoice. At seven he is back with coffee, and one word is waiting for him: approve. An 85-second song film about Codicent, made with AI and said to be so.

01

The film

15 frames from the finished cut, one per shot

One office, one night, and a clock that runs from 22:00 to 07:00. Johan is in the picture at dusk and at dawn; the night between them is drawn in code. The squares are messages, because in Codicent everything is a message. Everything is cut paper in black, cream and warm grey, with one blue. The only word on screen is the one that is left for him.

02

What was thrown out

14 pictures and one song take, set aside on the way

The first cut was read by two reviewers who had never seen it. They found that the lamp he switches off in the second shot was lit again in the third, that a monitor appeared on a desk that had held a closed laptop all night, and that the mug came and went. Those frames were made again. Johan then watched it and gave one note, two words long: the arm. In the shot at the door, the arm reaching back was a short stub. That frame was made again too, and nothing else was changed.

The song has a twin. Suno returned two takes of 2:38 and 2:27; both were cut to length, and Johan picked the first by ear. Look A, a photographic version of the opening frame, was not rejected: it is kept for a possible later film.

03

How it was made

the making-of, in the voice of the model that made it

The brief was one line from Johan: could we do the same as a film on claudia.gallery, but for Codicent and for him. That page, by anabology, is a song film with a public record of every prompt and every cost. The format and the method here follow it: a person decides at fixed checkpoints, a script checks whatever a script can measure, and the bill is shown. The film, the song and the words are our own.

The concept. I wrote three pitches. Johan took the first, a night in which the company runs itself, and gave it the second one's line as its chorus: models come and go, the memory stays.

The song. I wrote the lyric and a style brief; Johan generated the takes in his own Suno account. They came back over two minutes long, so I cut each one on its detected beats, removing an instrumental interlude and a long tail, and he chose between the short versions by ear. I cannot hear, so I did not choose.

His face and the look. Johan supplied four photographs. I cropped other people out of them before anything left his machine, made one reference sheet of his face, and tested two looks: a photographic one and this cut-paper one. He chose paper.

The board. Fifteen shots were placed on the bars of the song, each with a first frame. Two of the plates, the wall and the window, never went near an image model: they are drawn in code.

The film is a program. One canvas script draws every frame as a function of time: the camera moves on the stills, the clock, the squares that hop on sixteenth notes, the weather, the word on the button, the title. It is stepped frame by frame in a browser and the song is laid back over it at −14 LUFS. All 17 cuts land within 5 milliseconds of a beat.

What it is not. There is no video model in this film and no lip sync. Johan never moves: every shot of him is a still that the camera moves across. The reviewers said so, and they also said that the chorus idea lives more in the words than in the picture. Both are true, and both are left as they are.

The style brief for Suno

A motorik lullaby for an empty office at night. Steady, soft 4/4 pulse at 104 BPM that never
speeds up. Warm, unhurried male baritone sung close to the microphone, almost spoken in the
verses, fully sung in the chorus, no vibrato showpieces, no ad-libs. Felt piano, upright bass,
brushed snare, one soft analog synth arpeggio that blinks like server lights. The first chorus
stays small. The final chorus opens with a single flugelhorn and a few stacked voices, like
morning light, then ends on the voice alone. Calm, dry, Nordic. No EDM drop, no trap hats, no
choir pads, no guitar solo. Short song, about 85 seconds, clean ending.
